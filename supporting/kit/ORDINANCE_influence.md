# MODEL ORDINANCE: INFLUENCE AND GIFTS

*Repair 1, in the form a local body can lawfully adopt today. Gift prohibition, lobbyist registration, and post-service restrictions for local officials.*

**This is a drafting starting point, not legal advice, and it has not been reviewed by counsel.** Municipal ethics law is state-specific. Have your city attorney, county counsel, or an outside election-law attorney review any version of this before it is introduced. If you are qualified and you find an error here, say so in the open at the repository below and it will be corrected.

**What this ordinance does not do, on its face.** It does not limit campaign contributions or campaign spending. It does not restrict what anyone may say, write, publish, or organize. Those limits are not modesty, they are law: *Buckley v. Valeo* (1976) and *Citizens United v. FEC* (2010) place campaign expenditure largely outside what a local body may restrict, and the petition clause protects the asking. The full federal version of repair 1 requires a constitutional amendment. This version is the part that is available now, and it is ordinary, common, and durable.

---

## Before you introduce it

Answer these five first. Each one has killed a local ethics ordinance somewhere.

1. **Home rule.** Does your jurisdiction have authority to legislate on official conduct, or does the state occupy that field? Charter cities usually can. General-law cities and many special districts often cannot.
2. **State floor.** Most states already impose gift limits and disclosure on local officials. You are adding to a floor, not writing on blank paper. Check whether the state limit preempts a stricter local one or permits it.
3. **State lobbying registration.** If your state registers lobbyists, define the local register so it supplements rather than duplicates, and let a state registrant satisfy part of the local filing.
4. **Penalty authority.** Many jurisdictions cap the civil penalty a local body may impose. Find your cap before writing a number.
5. **Who hears a complaint.** An ordinance enforced by the same officials it governs will be read as theater. Identify an independent hearing officer, an existing ethics commission, or a contracted outside examiner before drafting Section 11.

## ORDINANCE NO. [____]

### Section 1. Title

This ordinance shall be known as the [JURISDICTION] Influence and Gifts Ordinance.

### Section 2. Findings

The [BODY] finds:

(a) Public confidence in local government depends on assurance that official decisions are made on their merits and not purchased.

(b) The residents of [JURISDICTION] are entitled to know who is paid to influence decisions made on their behalf.

(c) The movement of officials directly into paid advocacy before the bodies they recently served creates an appearance of, and an opportunity for, private benefit from public service.

(d) Speech, writing, assembly, and petition are protected activity, and nothing in this ordinance restricts them. This ordinance reaches transfers of value only.

(e) This ordinance does not regulate campaign contributions or expenditures, which are governed by [STATE] law and federal constitutional limits.

### Section 3. Definitions

**Official.** Any elected officer of [JURISDICTION], any member of a board, commission, or committee appointed by [BODY], the [chief administrative officer], department heads, and any employee who participates personally and substantially in procurement, permitting, licensing, enforcement, or land use decisions.

**Immediate family.** Spouse or domestic partner, parents, children, and siblings, including step and adoptive relations.

**Interested party.** Any person who, within the preceding twelve months or currently, holds or seeks a contract, permit, license, franchise, variance, grant, rezoning, or enforcement decision from [JURISDICTION], or who is a registered Lobbyist, or who employs one.

**Gift.** Any transfer of money, goods, services, travel, lodging, entertainment, loan on terms not available to the public, forgiveness of debt, promise of future employment, or anything else of value, for which the recipient does not pay full market price. Gift does not include the items listed in Section 5.

**Lobbying.** Communicating with an Official, on behalf of another person and for compensation, with the purpose of influencing a legislative, administrative, procurement, permitting, or land use decision of [JURISDICTION].

**Lobbyist.** Any person who engages in Lobbying and who, in any calendar quarter, either receives more than [$500] in compensation attributable to that activity or spends more than [eight] hours engaged in it. A person who lobbies solely on their own behalf is not a Lobbyist.

### Section 4. Gifts prohibited

(a) No Official shall solicit, accept, or agree to accept a Gift from an Interested Party.

(b) No Official shall solicit, accept, or agree to accept Gifts from any single source, other than an Interested Party, with an aggregate value exceeding [$50] in a calendar year.

(c) No Interested Party shall offer or give a Gift prohibited by this section.

(d) An Official who receives a prohibited Gift shall, within thirty days of discovering it, return it, pay full market value for it, or transfer it to [JURISDICTION] or to a charitable organization in which the Official has no interest, and shall report the receipt and the disposition in writing to the [Ethics Officer].

(e) The prohibition applies to Gifts given to an Official's Immediate family where the Official knows or reasonably should know the Gift is given because of the Official's position.

### Section 5. Exceptions

The following are not Gifts under this ordinance:

(a) Volunteer labor, including uncompensated work on a campaign, and anything else of value that consists solely of speech, writing, assembly, or petition.

(b) Items of nominal value not exceeding [$10] each and [$50] in the aggregate from a single source in a calendar year, including coffee, refreshments at a public meeting, and promotional items.

(c) Awards, plaques, and ceremonial items of primarily commemorative value.

(d) Meals and admission at an event where the Official is a scheduled speaker, panelist, or official representative of [JURISDICTION], where attendance is reported under Section 8.

(e) Reasonable travel and lodging paid by a governmental body, an accredited educational institution, or a professional association of governments, for attendance at training or a conference, where reported under Section 8.

(f) Gifts from Immediate family, and gifts given on account of a personal relationship that existed independently of and predates the Official's service, where the donor is not an Interested Party in a pending matter.

(g) Inheritance, and benefits available to the general public or to all employees on the same terms.

(h) Campaign contributions lawfully made and reported under [STATE] law, which are addressed in Section 6.

### Section 6. Campaign contributions not covered

Nothing in this ordinance limits, conditions, or reports on campaign contributions or independent expenditures. Those are governed exclusively by [STATE] campaign finance law and by federal constitutional limits. This ordinance addresses transfers of value to a person who holds or seeks office, and does nothing else. Any provision of this ordinance construed to reach campaign contributions or expenditures is void as to that construction, and the remainder stands under Section 14.

### Section 7. Lobbyist registration

(a) A Lobbyist shall register with the [Clerk] within fifteen days of first meeting the threshold in Section 3.

(b) Registration shall state the Lobbyist's name and business address, each client on whose behalf the Lobbyist acts, the subject matter sought to be influenced, and whether the Lobbyist held any position defined as an Official within the preceding [twenty-four] months.

(c) Registration shall be renewed annually and amended within fifteen days of a change in client or subject matter.

(d) A person registered as a lobbyist under [STATE] law may satisfy subsection (b) by filing a copy of the state registration together with the local subject matter and any prior service disclosed under subsection (b).

(e) No registration fee shall exceed the administrative cost of maintaining the register, and the [Clerk] shall waive the fee for any person representing an organization with annual revenue below [$250,000].

### Section 8. Quarterly reporting and public register

(a) Each registered Lobbyist shall file a quarterly report listing each client, the total compensation received for Lobbying in [JURISDICTION], the matters addressed, and the Officials contacted.

(b) An Official who accepts an item under Section 5(d) or 5(e), or who returns or disposes of a Gift under Section 4(d), shall file a report of that item within thirty days of doing so. An Official who has accepted nothing files nothing. No periodic filing is required of any Official.

(c) The [Clerk] shall publish the register and all reports on the [JURISDICTION] website, in a searchable and downloadable form, without charge and without requiring a request, within [ten] business days of filing.

### Section 9. Post-service restrictions

(a) For [twelve] months after leaving service, a former Official shall not, for compensation, communicate with [JURISDICTION] on behalf of another person with the purpose of influencing a decision of the body, department, or commission the Official served.

(b) The period is [twenty-four] months for a former elected officer, [chief administrative officer], and department head.

(c) A former Official shall not at any time, for compensation, represent another person in a particular matter in which the Official participated personally and substantially while serving.

(d) This section does not restrict a former Official from taking employment, from speaking on their own behalf, from unpaid advocacy, or from serving in another government position.

### Section 10. Employment negotiations while serving

An Official who is negotiating or has an arrangement for prospective employment with a person who is a party to a matter pending before the Official shall disclose that fact in writing to the [Ethics Officer] and shall be recused from the matter until the negotiation concludes and the disclosure is public.

### Section 11. Enforcement

(a) The [Ethics Officer or independent hearing officer], who shall not be an Official subject to this ordinance and shall be appointed for a fixed term removable only for cause, shall receive and investigate complaints.

(b) Any person may file a complaint. The identity of a complainant who is an employee of [JURISDICTION] shall be withheld from the subject of the complaint during the investigation to the extent [STATE] law permits, and retaliation against a complainant is itself a violation of this ordinance.

(c) A subject shall receive written notice of the allegation, access to the evidence, an opportunity to respond, a hearing on the record, and a written decision stating the basis for it. Decisions shall be published with personal information redacted.

(d) A person who discovers their own violation, reports it before a complaint is filed, and cures it within thirty days shall not be penalized under Section 12. A violation cured under this subsection is not counted as a prior violation for purposes of Section 12(a), and this subsection is available to any person once in a [three] year period.

(e) Findings are appealable to [court of competent jurisdiction].

### Section 12. Penalties

(a) A civil penalty of not more than [$500] for a first violation and [$2,000] for a subsequent violation, subject to any limit imposed by [STATE] law.

(b) Disgorgement of the value of a prohibited Gift, in addition to any penalty.

(c) Voidability, at the option of [JURISDICTION], of a contract, permit, or license obtained by a person who gave a prohibited Gift in connection with obtaining it.

(d) Referral to the [district attorney or state ethics authority] where the conduct may violate state law.

(e) No provision of this ordinance creates a private right of action, and no decision under it invalidates an otherwise lawful action of [BODY] except as provided in subsection (c).

### Section 13. Reporting and review

The [Ethics Officer] shall publish an annual report stating the number of complaints received, their disposition, the number of registrants, and any recommended amendment. This ordinance is subject to sunset review under [the sunset ordinance, if adopted] on a [eight] year cycle.

### Section 14. Severability

If any provision of this ordinance or its application is held invalid, the remainder and its application to other persons or circumstances is not affected.

### Section 15. Effective date

This ordinance takes effect [ninety] days after adoption. Registration under Section 7 is first due [one hundred twenty] days after adoption. Section 9 applies to Officials who leave service on or after the effective date.

## Drafting notes

**On Section 3, the Lobbyist threshold.** Set it high enough that a resident who calls their council member three times about a pothole is never a Lobbyist, and low enough that a paid contract advocate cannot sit under it. The compensation and hours test together is deliberate. A single number invites structuring around it.

**On Section 4(b).** The two-tier structure separates the person seeking something from the person who is not. A flat ban on all gifts from everyone reads as unserious and generates violations over sandwiches, which is how these ordinances lose public support.

**On Section 5(f).** The pre-existing relationship exception is necessary in a small town, where the contractor bidding on the water project has been the official's neighbor for thirty years. It is also the most abused exception in ethics law. Keeping the Interested Party carve-out inside it is what makes it survivable.

**On Section 6.** State this in the ordinance text, not only in a staff report. The first serious objection will be that this is campaign finance regulation by another name. Having the answer inside the enacted language, with a saving construction, is worth more than having it in a memo.

**On Section 9.** Twelve months is common and defensible. Longer periods for elected officers are common in larger jurisdictions. Anything above twenty-four months begins to draw challenges as a restraint on the right to work, and the specific-matter bar in subsection (c) is where most of the actual protection lives.

**On Section 11.** If your jurisdiction cannot fund an independent officer, contracting a hearing examiner per complaint is cheaper than a commission and preserves the independence. A self-enforcing ordinance is worse than none, because it converts a real reform into evidence that reform does not work.
