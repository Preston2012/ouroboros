# OBJECTIONS AND ANSWERS

*The attacks this project has actually drawn, with answers short enough to say out loud. Each one states what the objection gets right before answering it, because an answer that concedes nothing persuades nobody and because in most of these the objection is partly correct.*

**How to use this.** Read the concession first. If you cannot say the concession honestly, do not use the answer, because you will be caught. The last line of each entry is the compressed version for when you have one sentence.

---

## 1. "So an AI is going to govern me."

**What is true.** The architecture puts machines at the center of it, and there is no honest way to describe it that does not. Anyone who tells you the AI is incidental is selling.

**The answer.** The direction is the whole question. Humans decide. Machines sit in the audit seat, and the audit seat is pointed at the humans and institutions holding power, not at the public. The audit body publishes what it finds and may do nothing else with it. It cannot delay a vote, extend a deliberation, quarantine a campaign, trigger a revote, or label a source. Every response to a finding belongs to people. Twelve independently trained audit bodies check each other, and a council of ordinary citizens drawn the way juries are drawn can dissolve them.

**One line.** It is not AI deciding and humans complying, it is AI auditing and humans deciding, and the audit is aimed at power rather than at you.

---

## 2. "It was thirteen last month and now it is twelve. So the number is arbitrary."

**What is true.** It was thirteen, it is now twelve, and the change is logged in the document rather than hidden.

**The answer.** The number changed because the reason for it changed. Version 2.0 asserted thirteen. Version 2.1 states the requirements a count has to satisfy, capture tolerance, a quorum above two thirds, a floor, corpus independence, one body per major language family above a speaker threshold, and derives the number from those. The honest cost is printed alongside it: twelve tolerates three simultaneous captures where thirteen tolerated four. A document that revises a number and publishes what the revision cost is doing the thing you want a proposal to do.

**One line.** The number moved because it stopped being asserted and started being derived, and the document prints what the change cost.

---

## 3. "You want to verify everyone before they can vote. That is an ID database."

**What is true.** The architecture requires a check that a ballot comes from one distinct person, and any such check can be built into surveillance infrastructure. That risk is real and most proposals in this area do exactly that.

**The answer.** The subject of verification is the ballot, not the person. Five prohibitions are stated as binding and not suspendable by any emergency finding: no central registry of verified persons, no universal identifier, no linkage across contexts, no persistence of attestations, and no conditioning of anything whatsoever other than the counting of a ballot. Nothing else may depend on it, not benefits, not access, not services. Recovery procedures are biased toward enfranchisement rather than exclusion.

**One line.** It verifies that a ballot is one person's, keeps no registry of who was verified, and may gate nothing but the count.

---

## 4. "There is an emergency power in here. Every emergency power gets abused."

**What is true.** Yes. Every one, historically, without a clean exception. And this architecture claims a coordination power over governments that never joined it, for a short list of civilization-ending risks. That is coercion, and the document says so in those words instead of hiding it inside strong incentives.

**The answer.** It reaches jurisdictions and institutions. It never reaches a person. The engine holds no authority over anyone's body, movement, home, business, school, or place of worship, at any tier, under any invocation. Quarantine, curfew, closure, medical mandate, and travel restriction are outside its power entirely and stay with whatever authority your own law assigns them. That is written as a limit on what the engine is rather than as a policy it holds, so no emergency finding can suspend it. The power that remains is the power to make governments share information and take part in a joint response, it expires by default, its renewal escalates a tier, and personal liability attaches to whoever invoked it if the crisis turns out to have been overstated.

**One line.** It can compel governments to talk to each other and it can never compel you to do anything, and that boundary is not suspendable.

## 5. "The author sells AI software. This is a business plan in a toga."

**What is true.** He does. He builds and sells a memory engine and a personal companion, and the architecture names a personal companion as public infrastructure. That is a conflict of interest, it is disclosed on the front page of every document in this project rather than in a footnote, and you should weigh it.

**The answer.** Weigh it against three things. The architecture is written to be provider-neutral and says explicitly that if someone builds a better version of that layer they should win it. There is no organization, no funding, and nothing to join, so there is no revenue path that runs through your adoption. And the conflict was volunteered rather than discovered, which is the only version of a disclosure worth anything.

**One line.** He sells the thing, he says so first, and the document is written so that anyone else can supply it.

---

## 6. "Planetary governance. World government. No thanks."

**What is true.** Earlier versions of this document used the words planetary governance, and that phrasing invited exactly this reading.

**The answer.** It was changed, because the phrasing was both more threatening and less accurate than what the architecture does. It starts at community scale. It binds only communities that ratify it. It expands only where communities agree, and it has no military authority and no extra-legal claim of any kind. If nobody agrees, it governs nobody. The subtitle now says that out loud.

**One line.** It starts at the size of a town, it binds only what ratifies it, and it has no way to make anyone join.

---

## 7. "One human one vote. So anyone who walks in votes on my town?"

**What is true.** The phrase does sound like an open franchise, and this is a live grievance rather than a rhetorical trick.

**The answer.** One human one vote is a rule about vote weight, not about membership. It means no vote counts for more than another: no property qualification, no credential test, no productivity weighting. Who is on the roll is decided by each ratifying community through its own lawful process, and participating in the engine neither adds nor removes anyone's standing in the elections of the jurisdiction around it.

**One line.** It settles that no vote outweighs another, and leaves who votes to your community.

---

## 8. "Sortition. You want to replace elections with a lottery."

**What is true.** Sortition is used, and for a body with real authority: the citizen councils that can dissolve the audit bodies are drawn by lot.

**The answer.** It replaces nothing that is currently elected. The People vote, and their substantive will is the highest authority in the system. The single human office is elected. Sortition is used where the job is oversight rather than representation, for the same reason a jury is drawn rather than elected: a body that must judge whether power has been captured should not be recruited by the people most able to campaign. Selection is random, service rotates, and the seat has no career attached to it.

**One line.** Nothing elected becomes a lottery. The lottery is for the jury, and it exists so the watchdog cannot be campaigned into place.

---

## 9. "A Tribune. That is a king with a nicer name."

**What is true.** It is one person, holding a veto, representing the system externally, and able to initiate an emergency response. King was in fact considered as the name and rejected. Concentrating that much in one office is the most dangerous decision in the architecture, and the document names an unsolved gap in it: the five-layer defense protects against capture of the office and does not yet solve the office colluding with the branches it checks.

**The answer.** The office cannot legislate, tax, allocate resources, or sanction alone. Each of those requires the People and the lower-tier process. Its check on the People is procedural only: it can slow a decision, return it, force a re-deliberation, and it cannot override the substantive will of the franchise. One six-year term, never again, entry from thirty-five, mandatory exit near seventy-five. The emergency response it initiates does not take effect until the lower tier ratifies it. It is a broker with a delay button, not a ruler, and the unsolved collusion gap is printed in the document rather than left for you to find.

**One line.** It can slow things and speak for the system, it cannot legislate, tax, spend, or punish, and it gets one term and never returns.

---

## 10. "Why are six sensible reforms bundled with a forty-page AI architecture at all?"

**What is true.** This is the sharpest objection of the ten. The six stand entirely on their own, most people who want them do not want the architecture, and attaching them to it costs support. A pure reform campaign would drop the architecture and go faster.

**The answer.** They are attached because they came from it and saying otherwise would be a lie of omission, and because a hostile reader would find the connection in an afternoon and then the story would be the concealment rather than the reforms. So it is disclosed on the first page of everything. Then the practical part: nothing requires you to take both. Each repair is separable, separately sourced, and separately legal to adopt. Take one, take three, take the ordinance and throw the rest away. Nobody is tracking it and nobody has to be told.

**One line.** They are attached because they came from there and hiding it would be worse, and every one of them works alone.

---

## When you do not have the answer

Say that. This document is one person's proposal, it is wrong about things, and the strongest position available to anyone carrying it is that opposition in the open is worth more to it than agreement. If someone finds a hole, the correct response is to write the hole down and send it to the repository, not to defend the document past what it can support.
