# MODEL PROVISION: TERM LIMITS FOR LOCAL ELECTED OFFICE

*Repair 3, in the only form a local body can lawfully adopt. Term limits on local elected offices, with the federal question stated honestly.*

**This is a drafting starting point, not legal advice, and it has not been reviewed by counsel.** The qualifications of elected officers are the most state-specific area of municipal law in this document. Have counsel review any version before it is introduced. If you are qualified and you find an error here, say so in the open at the repository below and it will be corrected.

---

## Read this before anything else

**Term limits on members of Congress cannot be enacted by a state, a city, a county, or a school board.** *U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton*, 514 U.S. 779 (1995), held that states may not add qualifications for congressional office beyond those in the Constitution. Age caps on federal office are the same problem. Congressional term limits require a constitutional amendment.

A local resolution urging Congress to act is symbolic. Symbolic is not nothing, and there is a model resolution in Section B below for bodies that want it. But nobody should introduce it believing it limits anyone's term, and nobody should let it be reported that way.

This is the sharpest gap in the six repairs. Term limits are the most popular of the six, with 83 percent support in NPR/PBS/Marist polling fielded in April 2026, 78 percent of Democrats and 89 percent of Republicans, and Pew at 87 percent. The University of Maryland's Program for Public Consultation has measured it at 83 percent with no movement since 2017. And no legislature in the country can deliver it for Congress. That gap is the argument for why the amendment mechanism matters, and it is worth making out loud in the same breath as the local version.

**Cite Marist, Pew, or the Program for Public Consultation.** There is a matching 83 percent figure from a poll commissioned by U.S. Term Limits, an advocacy organization. The number agrees with the independent polling, which is exactly why you do not need it.

---

## The instrument question

**Term limits on elected officers usually cannot be adopted by ordinary ordinance.** In most states, the term of an elected office is fixed by charter or by state statute, and altering it requires one of the following. Determine which applies before drafting anything.

| Path | When it applies |
|---|---|
| Charter amendment by ballot measure | Home rule city or county with a charter |
| General-law procedure | Charter set by state statute, follow the state method |
| Board policy or bylaw | Appointed bodies, some districts and union locals |

For school boards, special districts, co-ops, and union locals, the governing instrument is the district's enabling statute, the bylaws, or the constitution of the local. Term limits in a union local are a bylaw amendment and follow the local's own ratification process, not municipal law.

Also settle these four before drafting, because each has produced litigation somewhere:

1. **Prospective or retroactive.** Does service before adoption count toward the limit? Retroactive application is more vulnerable to challenge and reliably generates a lawsuit from a sitting officer. Prospective application is cleaner and slower.
2. **Consecutive or lifetime.** Consecutive limits permit return after a break. Lifetime limits do not. Consecutive is far more common and much easier to pass.
3. **Partial terms.** Whether an appointment to fill a vacancy counts.
4. **Age caps are a separate and weaker case.** See the caution in Section C. Do not bundle an age cap into a term limit measure without a specific answer on your state's constitution, because it puts the popular provision at risk to carry the contested one.

## Section A. Model term limit provision

*For insertion into a charter, or adoption as a ballot measure, or as a bylaw amendment for a non-municipal body. Bracketed values are local choices.*

### Findings

The voters of [JURISDICTION] find that regular turnover in elected office broadens participation in local government, reduces the advantage that incumbency confers over challengers, and returns officeholders to the community they govern.

### Section 1. Limit

(a) No person shall serve as [OFFICE] for more than [three] consecutive full terms.

(b) A person who has served [three] consecutive full terms as [OFFICE] is eligible to serve again as [OFFICE] after a period of [one full term] has elapsed during which the person did not hold that office.

(c) The limit in subsection (a) applies to each office separately. Service as [OFFICE] does not count toward a limit on service as [OTHER OFFICE].

### Section 2. Partial terms

(a) The partial-term threshold is [one half] of a full term. This figure is stated once and used for both subsections below.

(b) A term served by appointment or by election to fill a vacancy counts as a full term for purposes of Section 1 if it equals or exceeds the threshold, and does not count as a full term if it falls below it.

### Section 3. Application

(a) This provision applies to terms beginning on or after [DATE], and service in a term that began before that date does not count toward the limit in Section 1.

(b) No person holding [OFFICE] on the effective date is removed from office by this provision.

*Alternative subsection (a), for retroactive application, which is a stronger measure and a harder one to defend:* This provision applies to all service as [OFFICE], whether before or after the effective date, and no person who has served [three] or more consecutive full terms as of the effective date is eligible for election to a further consecutive term.

### Section 4. Effect of ineligibility

A person ineligible under Section 1 shall not be nominated, shall not appear on the ballot, and shall not be appointed to the office for the period during which the person is ineligible. The [Clerk or elections officer] shall determine eligibility at the time of filing, and the determination is subject to review by [court of competent jurisdiction].

### Section 5. Severability

If any provision of this measure or its application is held invalid, the remainder and its application to other persons or circumstances is not affected.

### Section 6. Effective date

This provision takes effect [upon certification of the election] and first applies at the [general election of DATE].

## Section B. Model resolution urging congressional term limits

*This is symbolic. It changes no term and binds nobody. Adopt it if it is useful where you are, and say what it is when you do.*

WHEREAS the residents of [JURISDICTION] have adopted term limits for their own elected offices;

WHEREAS surveys conducted independently and repeatedly, including by the Pew Research Center, NPR/PBS News/Marist, and the University of Maryland's Program for Public Consultation, have found support for congressional term limits above eighty percent, with majorities in both major parties and across generations;

WHEREAS in *U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton*, 514 U.S. 779 (1995), the Supreme Court held that a state may not impose term limits on its members of Congress, and that such limits therefore require an amendment to the Constitution of the United States;

WHEREAS Article V of the Constitution provides for amendment by proposal of two thirds of both houses of Congress or by a convention called on the application of two thirds of the state legislatures, and either path requires ratification by three fourths of the states;

NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that [BODY] urges the Congress of the United States to propose an amendment limiting the number of terms a person may serve in the House of Representatives and in the Senate, and to submit it to the states for ratification;

AND BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that [BODY] acknowledges that this resolution is an expression of the position of this body and of the residents it represents, that it has no legal effect on the term of any federal officer, and that no provision of it should be represented otherwise;

AND BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that a copy be transmitted to each member of Congress representing [JURISDICTION] and to the presiding officers of the [STATE] legislature.

## Section C. Caution on age limits

Age caps poll nearly as well as term limits. Marist found 80 percent support for a maximum age for congressional candidates in April 2026, with 78 percent of Democrats and 83 percent of Republicans. The polling is not the problem.

The legal footing is weaker than for term limits and it is weaker in a way that varies by state. Qualifications for local elected office are often fixed by state constitution or statute, and a local minimum or maximum age can be preempted where a term limit is not. Some state constitutions contain their own equal protection or age provisions that a cap must survive. The federal Age Discrimination in Employment Act generally does not reach elected officials, so the exposure is usually state constitutional rather than federal statutory, which means the answer is different in every state and no model text can supply it.

The practical recommendation: put a term limit measure on the ballot on its own. If you want an age cap, get a written opinion from counsel on your state constitution first, and run it as a separate measure so that a challenge to the cap does not take the term limit with it.

## Drafting notes

**On three terms.** Two-term limits pass and produce a body that is almost entirely new every four years, which shifts power toward staff and lobbyists who stay. Three terms with a required break is the more common local settlement and the one easier to defend against the strongest objection to term limits, which is loss of institutional knowledge.

**On the break-in-service period.** A one-term break is a real interruption. Anything shorter is a formality that keeps a seat warm.

**On the strongest objection.** Term limits transfer knowledge and therefore influence to whoever is permanent, which locally means the manager, the clerk, the attorney, and the people who show up to every meeting for a living. Concede that. The answer is that this is a reason to pair term limits with the influence ordinance and the sunset ordinance rather than a reason to skip them, and that the alternative on offer is an incumbency advantage that has produced local offices with no contested election in a decade.
