# MODEL ORDINANCE: SUNSET REVIEW

*Repair 2, in the form a local body can lawfully adopt today. Automatic expiration of local ordinances and rules on a stated cycle, with renewal by affirmative finding against evidence.*

**This is a drafting starting point, not legal advice, and it has not been reviewed by counsel.** Have your city attorney or county counsel review any version before it is introduced. If you are qualified and you find an error here, say so in the open at the repository below and it will be corrected.

**What this is.** A body binding its own future output to expire unless it is renewed on the record. Colorado and Texas have operated statewide sunset regimes since the 1970s and both still run, so this is a tested American mechanism rather than a thought experiment. Sunset provisions were also routine in colonial and early state legislatures and fell out of use as legislatures professionalized, which means the argument for this is a restoration argument, not an innovation argument.

**The two ways it fails.** First, a body adopts it and then renews four hundred items in one omnibus motion, which is worse than not adopting it because it produces a paper record of deliberation that did not happen. Second, a body adopts it, ignores the calendar, and lets something load-bearing lapse by accident. Sections 6, 7, and 9 exist for those two failures specifically. Do not cut them for brevity.

---

## Before you introduce it

1. **What can a body bind?** A council can generally provide that its own ordinances expire. It usually cannot bind a successor's power to legislate, and it cannot sunset anything required by state or federal law, mandated by a charter, or promised in a bond covenant or executed contract. Section 4 excludes these. Confirm the list with counsel for your state.
2. **Count your inventory first.** Pull the total number of ordinances, administrative rules, fee schedules, and standing boards. If the number is over a few hundred, the staggered schedule in Section 8 is not optional.
3. **Name who writes the review.** The report in Section 5 is the entire mechanism. If nobody is assigned and funded to produce it, the ordinance becomes a lapse generator.
4. **Decide the default honestly.** Expiration on inaction is what gives this teeth. Section 7 provides one limited continuation so that a missed calendar does not become a public safety event. One, not renewable.

## ORDINANCE NO. [____]

### Section 1. Title

This ordinance shall be known as the [JURISDICTION] Sunset Review Ordinance.

### Section 2. Findings

The [BODY] finds:

(a) Local law accumulates. Provisions adopted to address a condition remain in force after the condition changes, and the cost of compliance falls on residents and businesses who had no part in the original decision.

(b) The ordinary legislative process creates no occasion to revisit a provision that no one is currently complaining about, and no occasion at all to repeal one.

(c) Sunset review, in which a provision expires unless renewed on the record against evidence, is long-established American legislative practice and is currently operated at the state level.

(d) Permanence should be earned against evidence and repeated, rather than assumed from the date of adoption.

### Section 3. Application

(a) This ordinance applies to each of the following adopted on or after the effective date, and to each existing instrument as it is assigned a review year under Section 8:

1. Ordinances of general application, other than those excluded by Section 4.
2. Administrative rules and regulations adopted by any department or commission of [JURISDICTION].
3. Fee schedules and rate schedules other than those set by contract or bond covenant.
4. Boards, commissions, committees, and advisory bodies created by [BODY].
5. Tax abatements, exemptions, credits, and other tax expenditures granted by [JURISDICTION].
6. Delegations of authority and emergency authorizations.

(b) **Default cycle: [eight] years** from the effective date of the instrument or from its most recent renewal.

(c) **Four-year cycle** for instruments in subsection (a)(2), (a)(3), and (a)(5).

(d) **One-year cycle** for instruments in subsection (a)(6). An emergency authorization expires at the earlier of one year or the stated end of the emergency, and may be renewed only by the process in Section 6, never by the officer exercising it.

(e) Every renewal is itself subject to this ordinance on the same cycle. There is no terminal renewal.

### Section 4. Excluded

This ordinance does not apply to, and nothing in it causes the expiration of:

(a) Any provision required by the [JURISDICTION] charter, by [STATE] law, or by federal law.

(b) Any obligation of an executed contract, bond covenant, interlocal agreement, or judgment, and any provision necessary to perform one.

(c) The annual budget and appropriation ordinances, which have their own term.

(d) Any provision defining a criminal offense, and any provision of the building, fire, electrical, plumbing, or mechanical codes adopted by reference from a model code or state standard.

(e) Any provision recognizing a vested right, a completed land use approval, or a final judgment.

(f) This ordinance.

### Section 5. The review report

(a) Not later than [twelve] months before an instrument's expiration date, the [City Manager or designated officer] shall publish a review report addressing each of the following:

1. The condition the instrument was adopted to address, and whether that condition currently exists, stated with whatever measurement is available.
2. What the instrument requires, of whom, and the estimated annual cost of compliance to the public and to [JURISDICTION].
3. Enforcement activity over the review period: actions taken, complaints received, and outcomes.
4. Whether a less restrictive or less costly means would address the same condition.
5. Any provision that is duplicative of, or in conflict with, state or federal law.
6. A recommendation to renew as written, renew as amended, or allow expiration, with the basis stated.

(b) Where the office cannot measure an item in subsection (a), the report shall say so plainly rather than estimate. An unmeasured provision is a finding in itself.

(c) The report shall be published on the [JURISDICTION] website not less than [thirty] days before the hearing under Section 6, and the public comment record shall be published with it.

### Section 6. Renewal

(a) [BODY] shall hold a public hearing on each instrument scheduled to expire.

(b) An instrument may be renewed only on affirmative findings, stated on the record, that:

1. The condition the instrument addresses currently exists.
2. The instrument addresses it.
3. The benefit justifies the cost of compliance identified in the review report.
4. No materially less restrictive means was identified, or one was identified and the instrument is amended to adopt it.

(c) **Each instrument shall be renewed by a separate vote.** No motion may renew more than one instrument, and no renewal may be adopted on a consent agenda or by reference to a list. A vote taken in violation of this subsection renews nothing.

(d) A renewal for less than the full cycle may be adopted where the record is incomplete, and states its own shorter term.

(e) An instrument not renewed expires on its expiration date. Expiration repeals the instrument. It does not revive any provision that the instrument had previously repealed or amended, and any such revival requires an express statement in an ordinance reenacting under Section 10.

### Section 7. Continuation for incomplete review

(a) Where the review report required by Section 5 has not been published, [BODY] may, by [two-thirds] vote and by a separate vote on that instrument alone, continue the instrument for a single period of not more than [one hundred eighty] days.

(b) A continuation under this section is available once per instrument per cycle and may not be renewed or extended.

(c) The motion shall state why the review was not completed and shall name the officer responsible for completing it.

### Section 8. Existing instruments and the transition schedule

(a) Within [twelve] months of the effective date, the [City Manager or designated officer] shall publish a complete inventory of instruments described in Section 3 that are in force, and shall assign each an initial review year distributed as evenly as practicable across the [eight] years following.

(b) Assignment shall place first those instruments that impose recurring compliance cost on the public, that have generated the most complaints, or that have had no enforcement activity for the preceding [five] years.

(c) The inventory and the assigned schedule shall be published on the [JURISDICTION] website and updated annually. An instrument omitted from the inventory is assigned the [eighth] year by default rather than expiring for omission.

### Section 9. Continuing provisions

(a) [BODY] may designate an instrument as continuing, exempt from expiration but not from review, by [two-thirds] vote and on published written findings that lapse would create a substantial risk to public safety, to the integrity of a public facility or utility, or to a financial obligation of [JURISDICTION].

(b) A continuing designation is reviewed on the same cycle under Section 5, and the review report shall state whether the basis for the designation still holds.

(c) A designation not addressed on review lapses, and the instrument returns to the ordinary cycle at its next review year.

(d) The [City Manager] shall publish annually the list of continuing designations and the findings supporting each. This list is the measure of whether this ordinance is functioning. If it grows to cover most of the code, this ordinance has been defeated by use.

### Section 10. Reenactment

An instrument that has expired may be reenacted only through the ordinary legislative process, and the ordinance reenacting it shall state that the instrument previously expired and the date. Reenactment starts a new cycle.

### Section 11. Annual report

The [City Manager] shall publish annually, and [BODY] shall receive in a public meeting, a report stating for the preceding year: instruments reviewed, renewed as written, renewed as amended, expired, continued under Section 7, designated continuing under Section 9, and the count of instruments scheduled in the coming year.

### Section 12. Severability

If any provision of this ordinance or its application is held invalid, the remainder and its application to other persons or circumstances is not affected.

### Section 13. Effective date

This ordinance takes effect [ninety] days after adoption and applies to instruments adopted on or after that date, and to existing instruments as assigned under Section 8.

## Drafting notes

**On Section 3(b), the cycle length.** Eight years puts most provisions in front of a body twice a decade and lands them under more than one administration, which is the point. Shorter cycles for rules, fees, and tax expenditures are where the real money is and where the least attention is currently paid. Do not set the general cycle shorter than the interval at which your staff can actually produce reports, because a calendar nobody can meet converts into Section 7 continuations and the mechanism dies quietly.

**On Section 6(c).** This is the load-bearing sentence in the ordinance. Every sunset regime that has failed, failed here. Expect the objection that separate votes will consume meetings. The answer is that a staggered schedule under Section 8 keeps the annual count small, and that a body unwilling to spend one vote on a provision it is imposing on residents for another eight years has answered the question.

**On Section 6(e).** The revival problem is real and technical. When an expiring ordinance had itself repealed an earlier one, silence about revival produces litigation. Keep the express statement.

**On Section 9.** Write the annual publication requirement in subsection (d) even if it feels redundant. The list of continuing designations is the honest measure of the ordinance. A jurisdiction that designates ninety percent of its code as continuing has not adopted sunset review, and the public should be able to see that in one page.

**On the politics.** This is the repair with no polling behind it. There is no national survey showing that voters want sunset review, and anyone who tells you there is should be asked for the source. What there is instead is the record: early American practice, two state regimes that have run for fifty years, and the PATRIOT Act surveillance provisions that carried sunsets, lapsed in March 2020, and did not produce catastrophe. Argue from that record rather than from claimed popularity.
