# THE CONVENTION WE NEVER HELD

*The working brief behind the six repairs. What the evidence for each actually is, where each one can legally be done, and full disclosure of what stands behind them. The one-page version is SIX_REPAIRS.*

---

## Where we are

Twenty-five percent of American voters say they generally trust the federal government. That is the lowest reading in more than two decades.

Among Americans under thirty it is fifteen percent, the lowest ever recorded in that survey. Half of them say people like them have no real say in what the government does. Two-thirds say elected officials act from selfish motives.

Those last figures split almost evenly across Democrats, Republicans, and independents. Whatever else divides this country, the sense of having no voice does not.

## The diagnosis

This is not a disagreement about policy. It is a system that cannot repair itself from the inside, because the people who would have to pass the repair are the people the current arrangement rewards.

## The claim

The founders did not believe they had finished. They wrote a mechanism for revision into the document itself and expected their descendants to use it. We have used it seventeen times since the Bill of Rights, and the most recent amendment that changed who holds power was ratified in 1971.

This is not a proposal to replace American government. It is a proposal to use the mechanism American government was built with: the convention this country was designed to be able to hold, and has never held.

A note on that, since the phrase has an existing owner. Article V provides two paths, congressional proposal and a convention called by the states, and the second has never been used and has real unresolved questions about scope. There is also an organized movement already working that path: Convention of States, which as of early 2026 has applications passed in twenty state legislatures of the thirty-four required, for a convention limited to fiscal restraints on the federal government, limits on its power and jurisdiction, and term limits on federal officials. Two of those three subjects overlap repairs 2 and 3 here. This document is not part of that movement, does not speak for it, and takes no position on it, and the overlap is named rather than left for someone else to discover. The claim here is narrower: the amendment mechanism exists, it was meant to be used, and structural repair through it is neither radical nor unprecedented.

## Six repairs

None of these belongs to a party. None requires you to agree with anything else on this page.

**1. If you want to change a law, you can talk, write, organize, and show up. You cannot pay.** No transfer of money, jobs, or goods of value to officials or parties. Petition freely. Influence that can be purchased is not speech, it is a market.

**2. Sunset every law and every authorization.** Nothing stays on the books by inertia. Renewal requires a fresh vote against fresh evidence. Permanence gets earned, repeatedly, or it lapses.

**3. Tenure and age limits on every office.** Entry windows and hard exits, all branches, no exemption for seniority.

**4. Pay for cures, not for management.** Public bounties for curing high-harm disease, results manufactured at cost. The current structure pays more for treating a condition indefinitely than for ending it.

**5. Credit reports that reflect who a person is now.** Recovery restores standing on the timeline of actual conduct, not a fixed schedule disconnected from it.

**6. Show people how their feed is built.** Any recommendation system operating at scale discloses what it optimizes for, and every person can inspect and change the shaping applied to them. This is about disclosure and user control. It is not about anyone deciding what you may see.

## What the evidence actually is

Stated honestly, because a claim of public support that does not survive a check is worse than no claim.

**Two of the six are among the most popular political reforms ever measured in this country.** Tenure and age limits: 83 percent for term limits and about 80 percent for age caps in NPR/PBS/Marist polling fielded in April 2026, holding across parties and across generations, with Pew at 87 percent and the University of Maryland's Program for Public Consultation flat at 83 percent since 2017. Lobbying: Pew finds 80 percent saying campaign donors have too much influence and 73 percent saying it of lobbyists, with 85 percent saying campaign costs make it hard for good people to run, at identical rates in both parties.

**Two rest on grievances both parties share, measured by polls that advocacy organizations paid for.** Cure incentives and platform disclosure both poll well. Every strong number on them was commissioned by a group that wanted the answer, one of them using a bipartisan polling team. That is worth something and it is not the same as independent measurement, and this document says so rather than hoping nobody checks.

**Two have no polling at all.** Sunset clauses have never been nationally polled, so the case is the American record instead: sunset provisions were routine in colonial and early state legislatures and fell out of use as legislatures professionalized, and Colorado and Texas have operated sunset-review regimes since the 1970s. Credit reform rests not on a poll but on the Federal Trade Commission's own accuracy study, which found 21 percent of consumers with verified errors, 13 percent with errors that moved their score, and 5 percent with errors severe enough to be denied credit or pay more for it.

Every source, with its weaknesses named, is in the companion sources appendix.

## Where each one can actually be done

This matters more than agreement, and getting it wrong wastes people's time. Three of the six can be adopted locally right now. Three cannot, and knowing which is which before you walk into a meeting is the difference between a win and a wasted year.

**Available to a city, county, school board, union local, or co-op today:**

- **Repair 1, in part.** Municipal gift bans, lobbyist registration, and revolving-door rules for local officials are ordinary, common, and legally durable. What is not available is anything reaching campaign expenditure, which runs into *Buckley* and *Citizens United*.
- **Repair 2.** A body can sunset its own ordinances and rules. There is state-level precedent to copy.
- **Repair 3, for local offices.** Term limits on a city council or county commission are common and legal.

**State legislature:**

- **Repair 5, narrowly.** The federal Fair Credit Reporting Act preempts most state credit-reporting law, but medical debt has been carved out successfully: Colorado banned its reporting in 2023 and Arizona voters passed Proposition 209. That path is proven.

**Federal, and constitutionally contested:**

- **Repair 6.** After the 2024 *NetChoice* cases, algorithmic curation is treated as expressive activity by the platform. The surviving path is compelled disclosure of factual operating information, which is narrower than a mandate. Worth pursuing, worth knowing the ground.
- **Repair 4.** Bounty structures and procurement reform are federal work.

**Requires a constitutional amendment, and no local body can do it:**

- **Repair 1, in its full federal form.** Gift bans and revolving-door rules are ordinary law. A flat prohibition on financial transfer to officials and parties is something else: it reaches the petition clause and it is a campaign finance restriction whatever it is called. Under current doctrine that requires either an amendment or a doctrinal change. The local version is available now; the full version is not, and this document does not pretend otherwise.
- **Repair 3, for Congress.** *U.S. Term Limits v. Thornton* (1995) held that states cannot impose term limits on members of Congress. Age caps on federal office are the same. A local resolution urging Congress is symbolic, which is not nothing, but nobody should mistake it for the thing itself.

So two of the six cannot be done by any legislature at all, and one of them is the most popular political reform measured in this country. That is the strongest argument here for why the amendment mechanism matters. The obstacle is the case.

## Two things the six do not cover

The six were chosen for measured cross-party support, and neither of the two things most visibly happening to people right now has polling behind it. They are commitments of the architecture rather than repairs on this list, and leaving them unmentioned would misrepresent what the project actually holds.

**Displacement.** Where a firm's automation eliminates positions, that firm carries the retraining and income-bridge cost rather than the public. No public money or tax preference flows to automation that cuts jobs, tested on measured headcount at twelve and twenty-four months against a pre-deployment baseline rather than on a firm's stated intent. Firms above a size threshold report automation-attributable headcount change publicly, because nobody currently knows that number at any useful resolution. And the architecture says plainly what it cannot fix: an income floor keeps a person alive and does not give them back a place in the world.

**Energy and siting.** Compute the architecture requires pays retail for power and water, takes no tax exemption, shifts no cost onto other ratepayers, and is built in no community that has not consented to it. Its footprint is published in absolute terms, estimated before construction and reconciled against measurement after. The estimate is in the document: roughly one terawatt-hour a year for the governance layers, roughly ten for a local-first companion layer, one to two orders of magnitude more if that layer were centralized instead, which is why local-first is written as a requirement.

Both are in section IV of the full document.

## What stands behind this, on the first page

These six are the near-term program of a larger proposal called Ouroboros: an architecture for governance built to audit itself on a schedule, starting at community scale and expanding only by consent. That architecture uses artificial intelligence. You are being told that here rather than forty pages in.

The direction it runs matters more than the fact of it. **AI does not decide. Humans decide.** Machines sit in the audit seat, where the work is catching capture and drift in the humans and institutions holding power, and where they are caught in turn by humans who can replace them. The audit body may publish what it finds and may do nothing else with it: it cannot delay a vote, extend a deliberation, or label a source. Every response to a finding belongs to people. Twelve independently trained audit bodies check each other, the size of a jury, and a rotating council of ordinary citizens, chosen the way juries are, can dissolve them. Every authorization expires by default. Every five years the whole structure audits itself in public and amends.

Machines appear in the design because the coordination problems in front of us move faster than any legislature has ever moved, and because the demand that somebody govern artificial intelligence is one of the few things Americans agree on across every line. This is a proposal for how. It is not a proposal to be governed by it.

## What is being asked

Nothing is being asked. Nobody is running this, nobody is recruiting, and there is no organization to join.

**And who does this?** Right now, nobody, and that is the honest answer rather than a modest one. There is no campaign, no staff, and no plan to build either. What has actually happened is that the document exists, it is openly licensed, its sources are published with their weaknesses named, and model language is being written. What would count as the next real step is somebody taking one of the three locally available repairs to a body they already belong to, and saying afterward whether it worked. If nobody does, the document stays in public and stays available, which is better than it not existing.

What exists is a document, in public, openly licensed, that anyone can take from. If one of the six is useful where you are, the section above tells you whether it is legally possible there and what has already worked. Model language is being published as it is written. If you think the architecture is wrong, the full document is public and the strongest thing you could do is say so in writing, because opposition in the open is worth more to this than agreement.
